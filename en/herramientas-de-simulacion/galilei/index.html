Galilei

The data model of the Chilean power sector, and the platform that makes it usable

Sector data is already public. The problem is that it sits across fifteen institutions, in PDFs, minutes and spreadsheets.

Why it exists

Pulling the sector's information together is a full-time job

None of this is secret: the Coordinator publishes operations, the CNE publishes prices and capacity, the SEA publishes projects, Congress publishes its proceedings. But each institution publishes on its own schedule, in its own format and with its own naming. Following the market means checking fifteen different sites, every day, and reconciling them by hand.

Scattered

Fifteen official sources, each with its own publication calendar, its own format and its own way of naming the same power plant.

Documents, not data

Between 300 and 500 official documents a day: letters, failure analyses, resolutions, minutes and environmental filings, nearly all of them PDFs.

Perishable

An online marginal cost is corrected to preliminary and then to final. A rule is amended. What was right yesterday no longer is.

No traceability

A spreadsheet that has passed through three hands can no longer tell you where each number came from or which document backs it.

Galilei does that work once, every day, and delivers the result as modelled data and citable documents.

Where it comes from

Ten official sources, one model

The platform draws on the institutions that regulate, operate and supervise the system, plus the sector's press. Every data point keeps the link to its original publication, so any figure can be traced back to the document behind it.

Ingestion runs daily. When a source corrects what it published —a price stage moving from preliminary to final, a rule being amended— the model records the correction instead of overwriting it.

The platform

Six ways into the same data

Six modules sit on top of the model. They are not separate products: they are six entry points to the same base, chosen by how much time you have and how specific the question is.

Daily Report

A system summary generated every morning: demand, marginal cost, renewable share and the news that moves the market.

AI Assistant

Natural-language questions on prices, operations, market and regulation, answered with citations and a confidence level. On the web and over WhatsApp.

Document search

Semantic search across the document corpora: Coordinator letters, SEA filings, Panel of Experts, resolutions, bills and press.

Dashboards

Supply, price and market boards, with actual versus scheduled marginal cost, geographic analysis and ancillary services.

Data Playground

The model's datasets, explorable and queryable in natural language or SQL, with charting and export.

Alerts

Keyword monitoring over the sources you choose, delivered by email at the frequency you set.

Coverage

What is there, and how often

Each domain is modelled at the grain its source publishes it in, not at a single one. Operations and prices arrive hour by hour; installed capacity is a snapshot; reservoirs are weekly. The matrix shows how many views sit at each intersection.

→ Scroll the matrix to see every column.

Grain matters more than it seems. A monthly average marginal cost hides exactly what you want to see: the zero-price hours at midday and the evening peaks.

One example

The same price, three times

Marginal cost is not published once. It comes out online first, within the day; it is corrected to preliminary two or three days later; and it closes as final —the settlement value— seven to ten days after that. The model keeps all three stages separately, instead of keeping only the last.

Intra-day

Online

The price observed while the system is operating.

2 to 3 days

Preliminary

First correction with metering consolidated.

7 to 10 days

Final

The settlement value, the one that gets invoiced.

That is why a query can ask for «the best stage available» and always get the firmest figure for each hour, or ask for one specific stage and reproduce exactly what was known at the time.

Integration

The model, inside your own assistant

A generic AI assistant knows about the Chilean power market whatever it can read on the open web: it stays at the surface. Galilei is exposed as an MCP connector —the open standard an assistant uses to plug into a data source— and gives it depth: the hourly series, the letters, the failure analyses and the versioned legislation. You do not connect it for convenience; you connect it because without it your assistant never leaves the surface.

In the assistant you already use

It installs as a connector in Claude or ChatGPT. The analyst asks in their own window and the assistant resolves it against the Chilean power market model.

No ETL, no scraping

Nothing to replicate, no downloads to keep from breaking. The connector queries the same model that feeds the platform, with the same freshness.

For your own builds

The same connector feeds the agents and applications your team builds: the model is available as a tool, not as a file somebody has to keep updating.

Auditable

Every answer can cite the view or the document it came from. The number does not appear out of nowhere: it can be traced.
