Why it fails

A model that optimizes the system cannot anticipate someone chasing returns

Planning has rested on centralized models: a plant enters when its investment is justified by the reduction in system operating costs. The problem is not the data, nor how those models are solved, but the assumption of who decides and why.

What the centralized model assumes

  • Investment is justified by the reduction in system operating costs.
  • Only what belongs to the least-total-cost solution gets built.
  • Entry happens at the optimal moment defined by the planner.
VS

What actually happens in the market

  • Nobody invests to minimize system cost: they invest for expected profitability.
  • Without a PPA there is no financial close, however «optimal» the project is for the system.
  • Timing is set by auctions, financing and portfolio strategy.

On top of that come assumptions of perfect rationality, full coordination and no market failures; and a technological and contractual homogeneity that ignores the diversity of preferences, contracts and practical constraints in the market.

How ABEX decides

It does not minimize costs: it simulates each agent's decision

The works plan is built by simulating each player's decision, with its own policies, instead of solving a single least-cost problem.

Market operator

Investing is assessed against market expectations: system operation and the future prices each developer expects, with congestion and zonal decoupling.

Generators and retailers

Each project is assessed by its effect on the agent's portfolio — profit and risk — not by its contribution to system cost. Asymmetric profitability across companies determines who builds.

Contracts as the driver

The PPA is the bankability condition: projects materialize when they secure contracts, not when an optimizer declares them efficient.

Real preferences

Technology, banking and auction preferences, learned from a decade observing the market.

Methodology

Three integrated modules

They reproduce the interaction between system operation, investment decisions and contract formation.

1

Electricity market simulation

An investment model produces the price and generation projections. This is where the market expectations that guide the agents are set.

2

Bid creation through the optimal portfolio

Each agent builds its energy and price bids with a portfolio optimization model. This is where individual strategic decisions on investment and contracting come in.

3

Supply contract award

Bids are matched, contracts awarded, and the new works enter the expansion plan. That is how the endogenous evolution of the market emerges.

Rolling horizon

Every auction is assessed with the information available at its time

For each auction, the Optimal Portfolio Model computes every company's bid: the minimum price (break-even) that covers the supply and keeps the project viable. If the company can cover it with energy already available, 5 USD/MWh are discounted.

What it delivers

Not just a works plan

Modelling contracts, portfolios and expectations also yields the commercial and price trajectory: one model for commercial, investment and regulatory decisions.

Commercial position

  • At what price will the next auctions be awarded?
  • How does my contract balance and spot exposure evolve?
  • When is it worth contracting and when to stay open?

Competition and investment

  • Which technology and which location are most competitive at each moment?
  • How do the others' strategies affect the contract price?
  • Where in the investment cycle are we entering?

Regulation and public policy

  • How does investment respond to a regulatory change?
  • What trajectory do tariffs follow?
  • What signal does the capacity market give?
